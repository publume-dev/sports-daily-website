- The Professional Footballers' Association has started legal proceedings against the English Football League over changes to League One salary caps.
- The EFL announced in May that Squad Cost Rules would replace the Profitability and Sustainability framework from 2026-27.
- Under the League One changes, clubs' wage spending would be capped at 50% of turnover, down from 60%; relegated clubs could spend 65% in their first season, down from 75%; manager pay is included; and no more than 50% of owner cash injections may be spent on wages.
- The EFL said it was "concerned and disappointed", while the PFA argued the introduction process did not comply with required consultation and agreement through the Professional Football Negotiating and Consultative Committee.
- A previous League One and League Two salary-cap plan was withdrawn in 2021 after an arbitration panel upheld the PFA's claim that the caps were unlawful and unenforceable.
- Oxford United are under a temporary registration embargo for failing to comply with the new rules and cannot sign players, including loans and free transfers.
- No hearing date for the proceedings, or effect on the start of the 2026-27 season, has been reported.
